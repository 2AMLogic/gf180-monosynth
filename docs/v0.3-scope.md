# v0.3 — the playable instrument, frozen

**Three notes and eight drums, on a real FPGA, with working controls and good
line output.** That is the whole of v0.3. The fourth oscillator is v0.4; more
drum families are v0.5. Nothing else goes in.

## The demo this release exists to make possible

Power it up. Start a groove. Play a bass line. Sweep the filter. Someone should
want to keep playing it.

The small speaker is for immediate fun. **The line output is where the sound is
judged** — a phone speaker cannot tell you whether the ladder is right.

## Scope, stated rather than implied

**Three notes, not four.** `model/voice_fx.py:533` builds
`[OscFx(...) for _ in range(3)]`, and `sounding()` maps held keys with
`range(3)`, so a fourth held key is **never sounded**. Three is the faithful
Minimoog number — it has three VCOs — so this is a deliberate freeze, not a
defect. Four-note paraphony is **unbuilt**, not merely untested.

**Eight drum circuits, not eleven.** BD, SD, LT, HT, CH, OH, CP, CB. Missing:
the mid conga/tom, the claves/rim shot, and the cymbal. The 808's sixteen
sounds are eleven or twelve circuits in five exclusive pairs, so the gap is
three circuits rather than eight voices — but adding them is v0.5.

**No voice is known-wrong.** `KNOWN_DEFECTS` is empty and the 808 acceptance
suite passes 53 tests under `TR808_STRICT=1`. The snare still separates from a
real machine at 3.4 knob-equivalents, which is a **known limit, not a tracked
defect** — the cause is unidentified and spread thin.

## What must be true to call v0.3 done

1. **One revision.** A tag, and every number in the release notes traceable to
   it.
2. **Integrated CI, green.** Not just block checks — the control link and the
   whole chip at its pins, each with a control that turns it red.
3. **The real engine on the board.** Not a placeholder. The build must contain
   `drum_kit`, `drum_dp`, `drum_regs` and `modal_dp`, and its provenance must
   say so.
4. **The right clock.** 12.288 MHz = 256 × 48 kHz, achieved and measured — not
   a 25 MHz oscillator with a timing constraint written against it.
5. **The host path complete.** MIDI in, kit initialisation, the **timed**
   coefficient writes the bass-drum attack and tom bends require, knobs on
   cutoff, resonance and drum decay, and a demo groove that plays with no
   keyboard attached.
6. **A capture checked against the model**, not merely a recording that sounds
   right.
7. **Board requirements written down** — clock, DAC, SPI host, pin count, and
   what a person has to buy.

## Why the host is in this list

The bass-drum attack window and the tom pitch bends are **timed coefficient
writes from the host** (contract 15.7.1), not RTL behaviour. A MIDI bridge that
loads the kit and forwards triggers will not reproduce those voices. The host
software is part of the sound engine, and the SPI link budget — bytes per
event, worst case when several voices retrigger together — is a design
constraint, not an implementation detail.

## What v0.3 does NOT claim

- **No sign-off physical verification.** ORFS's `gf180` platform ships no
  KLayout DRC deck, no LVS has run, and no routed netlist has been simulated at
  gate level. Reported DRC counts are the detailed router's own.
- **No demonstrated fit in a real padframe.** The routed ASIC result has
  `padcells: 0` — a bare core, not a shuttle template.
- **No hardware validation of the ladder.** The self-oscillation probe is built
  and calibrated (the discriminator is h5 relative to h3, separating our
  every-stage `tanh` from a one-tanh model by 25 dB) but 0 of 222 Legowelt
  recordings qualify. DR 0001 rests on circuit derivation alone.
- **Nothing is ratified.** The contract stands at revision 8 and all eight
  decision records still say *PROPOSED*.
