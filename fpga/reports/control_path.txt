# The production control path: a musical fixture at the pins (issue #81)

Generated from the runs themselves, at commit a932c7d.
Reproduce: `make -C fpga budget`, `make -C fpga host`, `make -C fpga host-controls`.

Nothing in this file is a recording. Every line is a bit-comparison of the
I2S wire against model/synth_top_model.py, with no tolerance, for control data
that entered the chip as 48-bit DR 0007 revision 2 SPI transactions.

==============================================================================
PART 1 -- THE RESULT
==============================================================================

  fixture   bar808 (fpga/fixtures.py): a bass line under a drum part --
            11 strikes, 4 gate/trigger events, 7 knob turns, 3 BD attack
            windows, 3 tom pitch drops. 288 transactions = 1 728 bytes,
            landing in frames 2..7480.
  path      fpga/fixtures.py -> fpga/spi_host.py -> 48-bit frames over CS_N,
            SCK and MOSI -> rtl-sketch/tb_top_bx.v's master ->
            rtl-sketch/synth_top.v -> BCLK, LRCLK, SDATA
  compared  8 378 I2S periods (175 ms), decoded from the wire the way a DAC
            does, against the model -- EVERY ONE IDENTICAL.

  Preconditions, asserted from the bench's own report and not assumed:
    datapath busy at a tick 0; overrun 0; link overflow 0; a sample strobed
    in all 8 379 frames; every I2S slot 32 BCLK; L == R in every period.

  THE ANTI-CIRCULARITY. The model was driven by frames fpga/spi_host.py
  computed BEFORE iverilog ran. For all 288 transactions the host's own
  prediction, the frame the CS_N PIN predicts, and the frame the write
  actually drained in are the same number. A scheduler that is wrong about
  the link therefore fails here even when the audio would have matched.

  That check earned its keep on its first run. The host's timing model had
  k_rst = 8, because rst_n_pad goes high at posedge 8. synth_top.v
  synchronises the reset through two flops, so the core's `cyc` counts from
  posedge 10. The model was right for 232 of 234 transactions and WRONG BY
  ONE FRAME on the two that straddled a tick. A scheduler checked only
  against its own arithmetic would still be wrong today.
  Wrong-then-right on this branch: 1 of 1.

  ==============================================================================
  SPI LINK BUDGET -- absolute numbers, contract 5.4 / DR 0007 revision 2
  ==============================================================================

  The frame is 20.833 us (48000 Hz, 256 cycles of 12.288 MHz).
  One transaction is 48 bits = 6 bytes: {F, 6'b0, SEC, A[7:0], D[31:0]}.

  link                             SCK    tx span    +CS gap     tx/s  writes/frame
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DR 0007 maximum 2.0 MHz      2.000 M    24.000u    24.326u    41109        0.8564
  tb_top_bx 1.536 MHz          1.536 M    31.648u    32.348u    30914        0.6440

    DR 0007 maximum 2.0 MHz: one write every 1.168 frames (24.326 us). A transaction is LONGER THAN A FRAME, so at most ONE write lands per frame
    tb_top_bx 1.536 MHz: one write every 1.553 frames (32.348 us). A transaction is LONGER THAN A FRAME, so at most ONE write lands per frame

    -> no burst arrives together. Two writes are 48.7 us apart at best (2.34 frames), 64.7 us on the bench link.

  ==============================================================================
  VERDICT: THE BUDGET HOLDS.
    The link cannot deliver a burst atomically and never could -- a
    transaction is longer than a frame. What #81 asks about is the two
    TIMED SEQUENCES, and both fit with orders of magnitude to spare: the
    BD attack window is 192 frames wide and needs 2.34 of them.
    The cost is not the deadline, it is SIMULTANEITY: a hit's setup writes
    have to be sent before the strike, and the host is what decides that.
